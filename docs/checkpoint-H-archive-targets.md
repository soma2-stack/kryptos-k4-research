## The single most valuable acquisition

> **One legible pre-1997 photograph of the UTC+1 face**, showing both the upper and
> lower aluminium bands with the names readable in order.

Why UTC+1 specifically:

- it is **Berlin's own face** — the sector a visitor photographing the clock is most
  likely to be standing in front of;
- it carries **19 names, ~137 letters**;
- **137 > 97**, so that one face alone supplies a complete 97-letter window.

That is the threshold. EXP-027 shows a **single** fully-transcribed large face
(UTC+1, +2 or +3) permits a Tier-1 restricted run of EXP-024 — not the whole drum.
The bar dropped from "reconstruct 24 faces" to "read one face".

## Acquisition request, in priority order

1. **UTC+1 face, pre-1997, both bands legible.** Tier-1 threshold on its own.
2. **UTC+2 or UTC+3 face** — each also exceeds 97 letters alone.
3. **Any frame spanning several European faces** — fixes multiple adjacency edges at
   once. The Flickr *Alexanderplatz, East-Berlin 1988* image is reported to show a
   wide run of sectors and is the best known candidate.
4. **The same face in two well-separated years** (e.g. 1984 and 1989) — tests layout
   **stability**. If stable, the whole 1970s–80s photographic pool becomes usable and
   the evidence base expands enormously. Currently UNTESTED.

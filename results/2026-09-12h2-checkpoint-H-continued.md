## 2 — Within-band order may be rule-determined

The mid-1970s UTC+1 upper band is transcribed as
`AMSTERDAM BERLIN BRUSSEL BUDAPEST MADRID PARIS PRAG STOCKHOLM WARSCHAU` —
**strictly alphabetical.** P(random) = 1/9! = 2.8 × 10⁻⁶.

All five multi-name groups in the 1984 and 1989 frames are also alphabetical
(ALMA-ATA/TASCHKENT, KRASNOJARSK/NOWOSIBIRSK, IRKUTSK/ULAN-BATOR,
CHABAROWSK/WLADIWOSTOK, MAGADAN/SACHALIN). P(random) = 2⁻⁵ = 0.031.

**The obvious worry is transcriber bias** — a human listing names from an image
often alphabetises. There is a clean discriminator: the **same** external agent
transcribed the modern lower band as `OSLO KOPENHAGEN WIEN BERN PRESSBURG BELGRAD
ROM TUNIS KINSHASA`, which is **not** alphabetical. So the agent does not
alphabetise by habit, and the alphabetical readings are most likely genuine order.

**Counter-observation, recorded not suppressed:** the readable historical UTC+1
*lower* subset `KOPENHAGEN WIEN BELGRAD TUNIS` is **not** alphabetical but **is**
strictly decreasing in latitude (55.7 → 48.2 → 44.8 → 36.8). The modern lower band
is likewise latitude-ordered, with 1 inversion in 8 against 18 expected at random.

**Current reading: upper bands look alphabetical, lower bands look geographic.**
Two rules on one face is odd, but it is what the data shows.

**Why it matters:** if a band's order is rule-determined, **order follows from
membership** — turning the acquisition requirement from "a fully legible ordered
transcription" into "enough legibility to identify which names are present".

**Grade: SUPPORTED, NOT PROVEN** — one external agent, unverifiable locally.

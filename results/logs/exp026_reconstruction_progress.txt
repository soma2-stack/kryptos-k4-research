## The sufficiency question: would a perfect name list be enough?
   EXP-024's preregistered procedures read each sector's UPPER band and LOWER
   band, in physical order. A name list gives neither. So suppose the 1989
   names per sector were known exactly - how many physical arrangements would
   remain consistent with them?

   zone      names   orderings k!      band splits 2^k
   UTC+1        19   10^17.1          10^5.7  
   UTC+2        15   10^12.1          10^4.5  
   UTC+3        15   10^12.1          10^4.5  
   UTC+8         9   10^5.6           10^2.7  
   UTC±0        10   10^6.6           10^3.0  

   TOTAL arrangements consistent with a perfect name list: ~10^125
   For comparison, EXP-024 as preregistered tries ~10^6 alignments.

   CONSEQUENCE, and it is the main result of this session:
   A NAME LIST ALONE CAN NEVER UNBLOCK EXP-024. Recovering every 1989 name
   would still leave ~10^125 physical arrangements, and guessing among them
   would both be computationally impossible and destroy the preregistration
   by unbounded multiple testing. The binding constraint is not WHICH NAMES
   were on the clock - it is WHERE THEY SAT: band and order within each
   sector. That is recoverable only from dated photographs of the object.

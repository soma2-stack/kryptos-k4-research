## EXP-001 — Forced crib keystreams · `replicated` + `negative`

index forced at each of the 24 confirmed crib positions and probe it for structure.

**Parameters.** 3 combiners (Vigenère, Beaufort, variant Beaufort) × 2 plaintext
alphabets (standard, KRYPTOS) × 2 ciphertext alphabets = 12 conventions. Probes:
period 1–48, affine-in-index over all 676 (a,b), arithmetic progression per crib,
required value range. No search, no fitting.

| Finding | Status |

| Crib conflict census: 9 ciphertext letters carry conflicts, forming exactly **10** conflicting position-pairs (same C, different P), and 12 pairs in the reverse direction | `replicated` — matches the inherited "ten conflicts" and pins its definition |
| No repeating key of period ≤ 48 is consistent at the cribs, under **any** of the 12 conventions | `negative` |
| No affine keystream `k[i] = a·i + b (mod 26)` fits, under any convention (0 of 676 per convention) | `negative` |
| Neither crib's forced key is an arithmetic progression, under any convention | `negative` |

**Derived artifact.** The full forced-key table is in `results/logs/exp001_crib_keystreams.txt`.
This is the table every future experiment should be checked against, and it did not
previously exist in this repository.

**Bounded-source lemma.** The required key range per convention falsifies, with no search
at all, any key source whose values are bounded below 25. Eight of the 12 conventions
require a key index of 24 or 25 somewhere in the cribs. Any proposal driven by a 24-hour
clock, a 23-lamp display, a 24-sector ring or a 0–23 counter is dead on arrival for those
eight unless it also carries a free additive offset.

## EXP-002 — Mengenlehreuhr lamp count as keystream · `negative`

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The `BERLINCLOCK` crib names the Berlin-Uhr at the
Europa-Center. If the clock is the key rather than a destination, the natural reading is
the number of lit lamps at a time of day advancing by a fixed step per character:
`k[i] = readout((t0 + step·i) mod 1440) + d (mod 26)`.

**Material difference from prior negatives.** The recorded negative "24-sector World Clock
geometry" concerns the Urania Weltzeituhr, a different clock; "World Clock tape as
conventional key" uses a city-name tape as a Vigenère keyword. Neither touches the
Mengenlehreuhr's lamp count.

**Parameters.** `t0` ∈ 0…1439 (1440) × `step` ∈ ±1…±60 (120) × readout ∈ {lamps_lit (0–23),
lamps_lit_sec (0–24), minutes_mod26 (control)} (3) × 12 conventions × offset `d` ∈ 0…25.
`d` is solved rather than searched: a candidate passes only if all 24 residuals
`k_required − readout` are equal, and that common value is `d`.

**Variants.** 6,220,800 parameter sets evaluated, covering a full space of **161,740,800**.

**Result.** 0 exact 24/24 hits. Best partial 10/24 (`lamps_lit`), which is at the null
level for a search of this size. Eight of 12 conventions were additionally excluded
analytically before any search, by the bounded-source lemma above.

**Conclusion.** `negative` for the Mengenlehreuhr lamp count as a fixed-step keystream,
at this exact scope. Not ruled out: the clock's lamp *bit vector* as a bitstream, or a
non-uniform time step.

## EXP-003 — Transposition composed with a short periodic key · `negative`

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** K1 and K2 are keyword Vigenères; K3 is a route
transposition. The natural next step is both. Under that model the forced keystream of
EXP-001 is meaningless, but after undoing the correct transposition it must become
exactly periodic.

**Material difference from prior negatives.** The recorded negatives for 3×31 routes,
4×22 routes and optimised columnar searches all gated on **plaintext readability**. This
gate is **key periodicity at the cribs** — a structural test that needs no language model
and cannot be fooled by a flexible scoring function.

**Parameters.** 97 is prime, so `i → a·i + b (mod 97)` is a bijection for every `a ≠ 0`:
the family is complete at 96 × 97 = **9,312** permutations, containing every decimation
and rotation, closed under inversion. Plus 848 rectangular routes (widths 2–48,
row/column/boustrophedon writes and reads, forward and reversed). × 12 conventions ×
periods 1–12 × two composition orders (key indexed on the plaintext side vs the
ciphertext side).

**Variants.** 10,160 permutations, **2,926,080** gate evaluations.

**Result.** 0 hits at the ≥ 8-constraint threshold (per-test false-positive rate
26⁻⁸ = 2.3 × 10⁻¹²). Best partial satisfaction never exceeded the chance level.

**Conclusion.** `negative`. The affine-mod-97 transposition family is now **exhaustively**
eliminated in combination with any repeating key of period ≤ 12, under 12 conventions.
This is a complete family, not a sample.

## EXP-004 — Running-key "mask" from the sculpture's own text · `negative`

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Sanborn has described K4 as using a masking technique.
Every periodicity test here fails, and a key as long as the message is exactly the
construction that defeats periodicity tests while leaving the cipher elementary. The
long keys available to the sculptor are the sculpture's own texts.
`k[i] = index(M[(o + dir·i) mod |M|])`, with both cribs satisfying one offset.

**Material difference from prior negatives.** The recorded negative "basic feedback /
self-masking ciphers, lags 1–12" uses the ciphertext at a *short lag* as its own key.
Here the key is an external fixed text at *any* alignment.

**Parameters.** 6 mask sources (K4 ciphertext, KRYPTOS alphabet, world-clock tape, K1/K2/K3
plaintexts) × every alignment × 2 directions × 2 key-indexing alphabets × 12 conventions
= **43,872** alignments.

**Result.** 0 exact hits; best 7/24. The score histogram tracks the binomial null
precisely: P(single alignment ≥ 7/24) = 2.414 × 10⁻⁵, expected count 1.06, observed 1.
This is noise to two significant figures.

**Caveat, stated plainly.** K1–K3 plaintexts in `data/mask_sources.json` are working
transcriptions marked `verified: false`; K2 is 370 letters here against a commonly cited
372, most likely from punctuation handling. The gate is fuzzy precisely so a transcription
error cannot manufacture a false negative — but this negative should be re-run once a
primary transcript is committed. Treat the K1–K3 rows as `inconclusive` and the
K4-ciphertext and KRYPTOS-alphabet rows as `negative`.

## EXP-005 — Toolchain validation · `replicated`

**Hypothesis.** A negative result is worthless if the apparatus cannot produce a positive.

**Method.** Synthesise ciphertexts from known constructions over a 97-character plaintext,
then hand them to the same code paths used by EXP-001…004 and check the method is recovered.

| Planted construction | Recovered |
| --- | --- |
| Period-10 keyword Vigenère (PALIMPSEST, KRYPTOS alphabets) | yes — periods 10, 20, 30, 40 reported |
| Affine keystream `k[i] = 7i + 3`, Beaufort | yes — unique fit (7, 3) |
| Running-key mask, aperiodic long key | yes — key IoC 0.0638 (English ≈ 0.066, random ≈ 0.038), no period |
| Transposition `a=11, b=5` + period-7 key, via EXP-003's gate | yes — **1** candidate survived out of 9,312 |

**Result.** 4/4. The negatives above are therefore not artefacts of broken tooling.

**Also delivered.** `k4lib/recover.py` — a method-recovery harness. Given any candidate
97-character plaintext it forces the full keystream under all 12 conventions and reports
periodicity, affine fits, linear recurrences of order 1–2, keystream IoC, and value range.
See `docs/ideas.md` § 1 for why this matters.

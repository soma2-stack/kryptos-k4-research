## Priority 3 — explaining the `+5` run, and its honest significance

**Method.** Scanned every lag 1–24, every difference and every sum, in both the
standard and KRYPTOS alphabets, forward and reversed, for runs of consecutive
positions satisfying a constant relation.

**Result.** Across **1,248** (alphabet, lag, delta) combinations, the `DIAWINFBN`
run is the *only* run of length ≥ 4 anywhere in the ciphertext. Correcting for
every start, lag, delta, operation and alphabet — and halving the count because a
difference run forward is the same run reversed, not an independent test — the
family-wise expectation of at least one run of length ≥ 5 is **0.0171**.

**Status: `inconclusive`, p ≈ 0.017.** Real but modest: roughly 2σ. Worth
following, not worth betting on.

**Mechanism analysis.** Under any shift cipher, `C[i+4] − C[i] = 5` expands to
`(P[i+4] − P[i]) + (k[i+4] − k[i]) = 5`. Both natural readings — a progressive key
over flat plaintext, or a flat key over patterned plaintext — are consistent, and
**neither is testable**, because positions 55–62 lie outside both cribs. The run's
only crib contact is its final relation `C[63] = C[59] + 5`, which yields one
equation in two unknowns and cannot discriminate between the readings.

**This is a hard ceiling.** The `+5` run cannot be resolved by crib algebra. It can
only be resolved by a mechanism proposed on other grounds that happens to predict it.

**Direct algebraic tests of the progressive reading, both negative:**

- No constant lag-L key difference `k[i+L] − k[i] = δ` exists at either crib for
  any L ≤ 8, under any of the 12 conventions. Simple progressive keys are dead.
- No linear recurrence of order ≤ 3 over Z₂₆ fits even EASTNORTHEAST's 13
  consecutive forced key values — let alone predicts BERLINCLOCK.

## EXP-006 — parameter-linear keystream models, exact · `negative`

**Key move.** Every model of the form "the key is a function of position" that
Priorities 1 and 2 list is *linear in its unknown parameters*. Solving instead of
searching gives exact solution counts and covers spaces far beyond enumeration: a
period-16 key alone is 26¹⁶ = 4.4 × 10²² keys.

`k4lib/modlin.py` solves over Z₂₆ by CRT through GF(2) and GF(13) — validated
against brute force on 300 random systems — and supplies `chance_solvable`, the
exact probability that a system with a given coefficient matrix admits any
solution for a random right-hand side. That is the degrees-of-freedom penalty,
computed rather than asserted.

**Scope.** 22,584 systems solved exactly: periodic (p ≤ 16), progressive (L ≤ 12),
polynomial (degree ≤ 5), each also with phase reset, index reset and additive
offset at **every boundary in a pre-registered neighbourhood b ∈ [48, 80]**, across
12 conventions. Out-of-sample gate additionally fits EASTNORTHEAST alone and
predicts BERLINCLOCK.

**Positive control.** A planted `progressive_reset(L=5, b=63, δ=7)` is recovered by
the same code path, both jointly and out-of-sample.

**Result. Zero fits.** The sweep's own false-positive expectation is 1.1 × 10⁻⁷.

## EXP-011 — the testability frontier · structural

**Part 1, alphabet-free period elimination.** Any periodic polyalphabetic cipher
gives positions `i ≡ j (mod p)` the same substitution. If a crib conflict pair is
congruent mod p, period p is impossible — *for any alphabets whatsoever*. This
holds for Vigenère, Beaufort, every Quagmire, every keyed tableau, and any bespoke
periodic scheme simultaneously.

**Eliminated: p ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 14, 15, 17}.**

**Part 2, falsifiability by parameter count.** 24 crib letters = 26²⁴ = 10³⁴ =
112.8 bits. Expected chance fits = |parameter space| × 26⁻²⁴.

| Class | Params | Expected chance fits | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vigenère family, fixed alphabet, period ≤ 20 | 10²⁸·³ | 10⁻⁵·⁷ | testable |
| Vigenère family, period 26 | 10³⁶·⁸ | 10⁺²·⁸ | **vacuous** |
| Quagmire I + period 2 | 10²⁹·⁴ | 10⁻⁴·⁵ | testable |
| Quagmire I + period ≥ 6 | 10³⁵·¹ | 10⁺¹·¹ | **vacuous** |
| Quagmire III + period 8 | 10⁶⁴·⁵ | 10⁺³⁰·⁶ | **vacuous** |
| Homophonic, 2 charts + free selector | 10⁸²·⁴ | 10⁺⁴⁸·⁵ | **vacuous** |
| Affine-mod-97 transposition + period-8 key | 10¹⁵·³ | 10⁻¹⁸·⁷ | testable |
| Affine transposition + Quagmire I period 8 | 10⁴¹·⁹ | 10⁺⁷·⁹ | **vacuous** |

**Quagmire I is closed** by pincer: periods 1–7 are impossible by Part 1, and from
period 6 upward the class is vacuous by Part 2. There is no period at which it is
both possible and testable.

**Anything pairing a free transposition with a free keyed alphabet is vacuous.**
Transposition work must fix the alphabet in advance.

## EXP-012 — fractionation closed · `negative` + undecidable

**Part 1.** All 26 letters occur in K4. Any cipher with a smaller output alphabet
is therefore impossible: **Playfair, bifid, two-square, four-square, letter-form
Nihilist, straddling checkerboard, ADFGX, ADFGVX, Baconian, and every I/J-merged
or 25-letter scheme.** Concretely, K4 contains `J` at positions 40, 51 and 81.
P(all 26 appear in 97 uniform draws) = 0.54, so this is an ordinary property of the
text that happens to be an exact eliminator.

**Part 2.** A block cipher only receives crib information from blocks lying
*wholly* inside a crib. Counting them against a 27-cell trifid cube (10²⁸·⁰):
periods 2 and 3 are testable; **period 4 and above are vacuous** (24 crib letters
fall to as few as 0 usable constraints).

**General lesson: crib evidence does not survive block boundaries.** Count covered
positions before searching any block-structured cipher.

**Methodological note worth keeping.** A first implementation processed blocks in
text order, hit a 40,000,000-node cap on three of five runs, and returned no
solutions. That is *heuristic failure, not elimination*, and reporting it as a
negative would have been wrong. Re-ordering to most-constrained-block-first — take

blocks act as pure checks — cut the total to **657,192 nodes**, a 60-fold
reduction, and made every run complete. The elimination is real only because the
search finishes.

With EXP-012 (periods 4+ undecidable) this closes fractionation entirely.

## EXP-015 — relative phase between cribs · `negative`

**Gap this closes.** EXP-006 scanned resets only in the pre-registered window
b ∈ [48, 80], chosen because the position-63 lead pointed there. The cribs are
separated by positions 34–62, so a reset, an inserted null, a dropped character or
a key that began counting elsewhere could sit outside that window.

**Reparameterisation.** Everything a single break between the cribs can do to a
periodic key is one extra discrete parameter — the relative phase `δ` with which
the key re-enters at the second crib. Enumerating `δ` over 0..p−1 covers *every*
break position at once, at cost p instead of 96, and additionally covers nulls,
dropped characters, and K4 being the tail of one continuous sculpture-long
message whose key phase at position 0 is not 0.

**Scope.** 43,836 systems: relative phase (p ≤ 22) with and without an additive
offset; progressive keys with relative phase; plus the **full 1..96 boundary
sweep**, lifting EXP-006's window entirely. Planted control recovered.
**Zero fits.**

**Honest limit.** Period 21 leaves 3 spare constraints and period 22 only 2
(chance 26⁻² = 1.5 × 10⁻³, expected 0.74 false fits across ~500 tests). Period 22
is **marginal** and period 23 upward is vacuous: 24 crib letters cannot decide a
key as long as the evidence itself.

## EXP-016 — complete transposition family x every keystream model · `negative`

Closes the cross product `next-steps.md` § 2 identified as the largest surviving
testable family. EXP-003 had tested the complete affine-mod-97 permutation family
against periodic keys only; the progressive, polynomial and periodic models built
since had only been tested at the identity permutation.

**Why this is testable when other transposition work is not.** EXP-011 shows
transposition plus a *free keyed alphabet* is vacuous (10⁺⁷·⁹ expected chance fits)
while transposition plus a *fixed* alphabet is comfortably testable (10⁻¹⁸·⁷). The
12 conventions are therefore declared in advance and nothing about them is fitted.

**Engineering note.** The model's coefficient matrix does not depend on the
permutation — only the right-hand side does. Factoring each model once via
`modlin.make_consistency_checker` (validated on 7,000 random cases) turns an exact
solve into a few dot products, which is what makes 4.1M evaluations tractable.

**Scope.** 10,160 permutations (9,312 affine mod 97, complete and closed under
inversion, plus 848 rectangular routes) × 12 conventions × 25 models (progressive
L ≤ 12, polynomial degree ≤ 5, periodic 8 ≤ p ≤ 16) = **4,145,280 gate
evaluations**. Planted `progressive(L=5, δ=7)` under permutation a=11, b=5
recovered by the same checker. **Zero fits**; the sweep's own false-positive
expectation is 2.0 × 10⁻⁵.

**Declared gap.** Order B (key indexed on the ciphertext side) is covered at every
permutation for the periodic models via an O(24) residue check, but for the
progressive and polynomial models the coefficient matrix varies with the
permutation and cannot be pre-factored. Order B for those **remains untested** and
is recorded as such, not as a negative.
